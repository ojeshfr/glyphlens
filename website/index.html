Read any Chinese screen in English. Offline.

GlyphLens reads the text on a window or display with a local OCR, then shows the translation in a small panel that floats above your other apps. Nothing from your screen leaves your PC.

See how it works Read the privacy details

Simplified and Traditional Chinese to English. Built and tested on Windows 11.

Three steps, all on your PC

  1. 1

    Pick what to read

    Choose a window or a whole display from a list and press Start. Capture runs only while you want it to.

  2. 2

    The text is read locally

    A local OCR (PP-OCRv5) reads the Chinese in a worker process on your computer. Lines of one chat message are merged back into one message before they are translated.

  3. 3

    The translation floats above your apps

    A small panel shows each original with its English translation. Pin it, make it click-through, copy either text, and keep working in the app underneath.

Made for reading Chinese on a PC

Chinese first

Simplified and Traditional Chinese go to English. When Chinese is what you translate, GlyphLens chooses the local Chinese OCR by itself and never overrides a choice you made.

A panel that stays out of the way

It floats above other windows without taking focus. In click-through mode your mouse and keyboard pass straight to the app underneath, and a small handle brings the panel back.

Live reading that only re-reads what changed

For a chat that keeps growing, only the changed part of the screen is read again. On our test PC a Live pass took a median of 142 ms, against 733 ms when every frame was read in full.

One run on one PC (Intel Core i7-12700H, no GPU). A measurement, not a benchmark.

Honest about its state

The panel says what is happening: Live, Paused, Capture stopped, Model missing. Green appears only when the worker answers and a screen is being read.

Light and dark

Follows Windows, or stays light or dark as you choose. Text size and opacity of the panel are yours to set.

Updates you control

GlyphLens can check for a new version about once a day. Every update is signed, nothing is installed without your click, and the check can be turned off.

Your screen stays on your PC

GlyphLens is built so that what you read is never the thing that travels. Here is exactly what happens, and what is not proven.

What stays on your PC

  • Screenshots and recognised text live in memory only. They are never written to disk or logged.
  • OCR and translation run in a local worker process that is forbidden to open network connections.
  • Translation models are files you put on your PC. GlyphLens never downloads them.
  • It remembers only the panel's position, size, opacity and text size, your appearance choice, and the update settings.

What uses the network

  • The update check, about once a day, and only if you leave it on. It sends the request itself and nothing from your screen.
  • An update installer is downloaded only when you press Download, and it is checked against a signed SHA-256 before it can run.
  • No account, no sign-in, no analytics.

How we check it

The Overview page lists the network connections owned by GlyphLens and its worker whenever you ask. In our own test run, 28 samples over 61 seconds of Live reading and translation found no remote connection and no open UDP endpoint.

Sampling can miss a connection that opens and closes between two samples. We have not captured packets or blocked GlyphLens with a firewall, so this is evidence, not proof.

Where it stands

Not released yet

There is no installer to download today

GlyphLens is an early preview. This page will carry the download link when a signed installer exists. The code has no distribution licence yet, so it is not offered for redistribution.

What the preview needs

  • Windows 11, 64-bit. Tested on build 26200. Windows 10 and other display scales are not tested.
  • A local worker environment. The worker runs on Python, which you set up once.
  • Two model sets you download yourself. The PP-OCRv5 OCR folders and an Argos Translate Chinese-to-English package (CC-BY 4.0). GlyphLens never fetches models for you.

Questions

Does it work without an internet connection?

Yes. Reading and translating need no network. Only the optional update check does.

How good are the translations?

The translation model is Argos Translate, a compact model that runs on a CPU. On our own 52-sentence test it gets the meaning across, with a median of 72 ms per sentence, and it is not a human translator. The panel keeps the original next to the translation so you can check it.

Why are the models not included?

Each model has its own licence and size, and you stay in control of what is on your PC. GlyphLens reports clearly which model is missing and where to put it.

Which languages are supported?

Simplified and Traditional Chinese to English. Other languages are not offered until they have been tested properly.

Is there a macOS version?

No. The look is inspired by Apple's design, but GlyphLens is a Windows app.
